# Frames, bodies and measurement: design

Date: 2026-11-05. For `packages/next` (the Cesium 2.0 WebGPU engine).
Extends CESIUM2_WEBGPU_PLAN.md §13.14 ("Bodies, frames and engineering
coordinates"), which this document turns into a buildable design. Calls
marked **(builder's call)** were made by the author of this document, not
decided with the product owner; everything else was agreed.

## 1. Intent

Two kinds of users need the engine to know which coordinate frame every
position is in, and to measure exactly across frames:

- **Design users.** A design user looks at a globe of all their models
  around the world. Each was designed in its own local or
  projected coordinates. A long infrastructure model (a highway many miles
  long) must sit on the Earth's curved surface wherever the camera goes, and
  measurements on the displayed model must agree with the design's own
  coordinates, as the CAD tool would report them, with the true distance
  over the Earth shown beside them.
- **Multi-body users.** The same must work on other bodies (a lunar base in
  its own site grid, fit to the Moon) and across bodies: the distance from an
  antenna on a structure on the Moon to one on the Earth, or to a spacecraft
  or satellite, at an instant, with its light time, accurate to the meter.

Two gallery examples exercise and show this off:

1. **Design coordinates on the globe**: a portfolio of design models on the
   globe, including a long synthetic highway corridor, measured in design
   and true terms.
2. **Earth to Moon: Artemis II**: the April 2026 flight with the Earth's and
   the Moon's tilesets together, cameras that follow Orion or fly freely,
   coordinates read in several frames, and a communications overlay whose
   job is to show measurement accuracy.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

### Goals

- A frame tree in the public API: ICRF, each body's fixed frame, local
  frames, engineering frames. Values carry their frame; conversion is an
  explicit call at a time, never an implicit reuse of a bare matrix.
- Time as a first-class input wherever more than one body is involved.
- Measurement and snapping as engine calls, not only widgets: instantaneous
  distance and light time fit naturally over the public API.
- Meter-level-or-better accuracy for cross-body geometry and light time.
- Design-coordinate measurements that agree with the CAD data.
- Long design models displayed fit to the body's curvature.
- Both examples, with synthetic data and real public data.

### Non-goals

- Not a mission-planning tool: no orbit propagation, maneuver design or
  scheduling. Trajectories come from published ephemerides.
- The comms overlay demonstrates accuracy; it is not a link-analysis
  product. Link quality is a stated model.
- The globe (terrain and imagery tiling) stays Earth-only. The Moon is a 3D
  Tiles tileset (Cesium Moon), not a globe **(builder's call, consistent with
  §13.14)**.
- Reading alignments out of real iModels or IFC 4.3 files (later; the
  synthetic corridor defines its alignment directly).
- Apparent-position rendering (light time and aberration applied to what is
  drawn). The scene is drawn geometrically at the view's instant; apparent
  directions are available to measurement and pointing **(builder's call)**.

### Anti-goal (the failure that matters most)

- **A measured value that disagrees with the underlying CAD data.** However
  good the globe looks, a design distance, station, offset or elevation that
  doesn't match what the design tool reports is a failure.

Secondary anti-goals: a result that claims more accuracy than its inputs
have (a TLE orbit presented as meter-accurate), and a demo whose plumes or
plasma look like a game's while claiming to be physical.

## 3. Constraints

- **Precision:** float64 on the CPU throughout; the GPU only ever sees
  camera-relative values (the existing hi/lo and camera-relative scheme,
  plan §13.15). Hi/lo is valid to ±2^31 m, which covers Earth-Moon.
- **Browser only:** no server-side computation. Data the engine needs is
  self-hosted (none of JPL, NAIF or IERS send CORS headers; CelesTrak does
  for EOP).
- **Data lives outside the repo.** Everything new (the ephemeris and
  orientation excerpts, EOP, leap seconds, PROJ's database and grids, the
  trajectories, the corridor's tilesets, models) is fetched or generated by
  `npm run setup` into a store outside the repository, as the Slang WASM
  build already is (`~/.cesium-bench/…`), and served by the dev server. The
  engine takes the data's location as configuration, so library users point
  it at their own copy; nothing new is committed or shipped in the package.
- **Size:** heavy dependencies load only when needed, as the Slang WASM
  compiler does: PROJ only when an engineering frame needs exact transforms;
  ephemeris data only when a scene uses time-dependent frames beyond today's
  sun and moon.
- **Licenses** (section 9): redistribute only what may be redistributed;
  rename and record provenance for any excerpted NAIF kernel; credit NASA,
  IERS, IOGP/EPSG and CC-BY authors.
- **No ion tokens in the repo.** Examples take a token from `.env.local`,
  the Viewer's UI, or ion's default evaluation token.
- **Repository practice:** commit locally, never push (the owner hands work
  off with a git bundle); Snyk scans on new code; Slang for engine shaders,
  WGSL or Slang for custom shaders; never rewrite compiler output.

## 4. Accuracy targets and budget

Target: **1 m or better** for cross-body positions, ranges and light-time
ranges (Earth surface to Moon surface, to Orion, to a lunar orbiter); **well
under 1 mm** for design-coordinate round trips; design measurements exact to
the input data's own precision.

Known contributions (from the recon, 2026-11-05) the design must handle:

| Source of error | Size if ignored | Handled by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Analytic Sun/Moon series (today's Meeus / Simon 1994) | km | DE440 excerpt |
| Earth orientation: UT1 (1 ms) | 0.46 m at the surface, ~28 m at the Moon | EOP table (UT1−UTC to ~35 µs) |
| Earth orientation: precession-nutation, IAU 2000B vs 2000A | ~1.9 m at the Moon | IAU 2006/2000A from ERFA, plus EOP dX/dY |
| Polar motion | up to ~15 m at the surface | EOP table |
| TT vs TDB confusion | ~1.7 m geocentric Moon, ~50 m barycentric | proper time scales |
| TT/TDB coordinate scaling (IERS 2010 eq. 11.19) | ~9.5 m plus up to ~2 m Lorentz term | applied to geocentric vectors |
| Solar Shapiro delay on light time | ~7.5 m one way | added to light time |
| Single-iteration light time | ~4 m | converged (CN) light time |
| EME2000 vs ICRF frame bias (~23 mas) | ~45 m at 400,000 km | frame-bias rotation for EME2000 inputs |
| Moon frame: MOON_PA vs MOON_ME | ~875 m at the surface | content declares its frame; Cesium Moon assumed ME |
| Moon frame: IAU_MOON series vs DE440 ME | ~155 m | DE440 lunar PCK, not the IAU series |
| Rigid placement of a design model | 7.8 m sag at 10 km, 31 m at 20 km | per-vertex reprojection, or exact inverse for measurement |
| US survey vs international foot | 2 ppm (~10 cm at 50 km) | units from the CRS definition |
| Tessellation (a click on a chord of a tiled arc) | mm to cm | snapping to source geometry, or a stated tolerance |

Each measurement result carries an accuracy statement derived from its
inputs (section 6.5).

## 5. Time and ephemerides

**Time.** A new `Instant` type, convertible to and from UTC (the `Clock`
keeps its UTC milliseconds), TAI, TT, TDB and UT1:

- Leap seconds from a bundled table (NAIF LSK or IERS Bulletin C, ~1 KB),
  with its expiry date checked.
- TDB−TT from Fairhead & Bretagnon as in ERFA `eraDtdb` (ns level).
- UT1−UTC and polar motion from the EOP table.
- Representation: float64 seconds past J2000 TT gives ~0.1 µs resolution
  over this century, ample for 1 m (Earth's surface moves 0.46 mm per µs)
  **(builder's call; use a two-part form if a test shows a need)**.

**Ephemerides.** A TypeScript reader for SPICE SPK type 2 (Chebyshev) and
binary PCK type 2 (Euler angles), about 200 lines each, validated against
jplephem (MIT) reference vectors generated offline and checked in.

- **Planetary data:** an excerpt of DE440 with the Sun (10 wrt 0), the
  Earth-Moon barycenter (3 wrt 0) and the Moon (301 wrt 3); the Earth is
  derived from the Moon with EMRAT. 43.8 KB per year; default span
  2000–2050 (~2.2 MB) **(builder's call; the span is a setup parameter)**.
  Setup fetches `de440s.bsp` (or only its needed blocks) and writes the
  excerpt into the data store.
- **Lunar orientation:** an excerpt of `moon_pa_de440_200625.bpc` (11.7 KB
  per year) plus the fixed PA→ME rotation from `moon_de440_250416.tf`
  (TKFRAME 67.8526″, 78.6944″, 0.2785″ about axes 3, 2, 1). MOON_ME is
  MOON_ME_DE440_ME421, the frame of LOLA and most lunar cartography.
- **The Sun's position for lighting** also comes from DE440 when the data is
  loaded, so the light and the measurements agree; Meeus remains the
  fallback without data.

**Earth orientation.** IAU 2006/2000A, CIO-based (X, Y, s, Earth rotation
angle, polar motion), ported from ERFA (BSD-3) to TypeScript, with EOP
dX/dY. Validated against ERFA test values and Skyfield/astropy vectors
generated offline. ITRF is treated as WGS84 (they agree to centimeters).
`icrfToFixed` (today: IAU 1976 precession plus GMST, no nutation) is
replaced by this, behind the same name for its existing users.

**EOP data.** A daily table: UT1−UTC, x, y, dX, dY. Source: CelesTrak's
EOP files (CORS) or a self-hosted repack of IERS finals2000A / C04
(~40 bytes a day). Predictions are flagged; an instant outside the table
falls back to predictions or zero corrections with a degraded accuracy
statement.

## 6. The frame tree

### 6.1 Frames

A `Frame` is a node with a parent and a float64 transform to its parent as a
function of time (constant for rigid frames). Kinds:

- **Inertial:** ICRF (BCRS axes, origin the solar system barycenter), the
  root. Derived inertial frames for readouts: Earth-centred inertial (GCRF
  axes at the geocenter), and a Sun-centred frame **(builder's call: ICRF
  axes at the Sun; an ecliptic variant if the readout wants it)**.
- **Body-fixed:** each `Body` gets its fixed frame: Earth ITRF (from §5's
  Earth orientation), Moon ME (from the lunar PCK). Placed under ICRF from
  the ephemerides at the instant.
- **Local rigid:** east-north-up at a place, a tileset root, a model, a
  node, a placement. The existing transform table (`World.addTransform`,
  parent × local in float64) becomes a subtree under a body frame. Content
  that names no frame is Earth-fixed, so Earth-only code keeps working.
- **Engineering:** a design's coordinate system attached to a body
  (section 7). The mapping to the body is nonlinear (a map projection plus
  a vertical datum), resolved by PROJ, with an optional rigid site transform
  (a local site grid within the projected system).

### 6.2 Values in the API

Positions and transforms carry their frame at run time, because cross-body
conversion happens at run time. A frame-tagged point holds its frame, its
float64 coordinates and, for points that move, the instant they're valid at.
Conversion is explicit: convert a point to another frame at an instant, or
get the transform between two frames at an instant. Compile-time branding
(so an Earth-fixed position can't be passed where a design point is
expected) is added where it's cheap **(the exact type names and shapes are
the builder's call; the plan's sketches were `frame.toFixed(p)`,
`a.transformTo(b)` and `Fixed<Vec3>`)**.

Existing APIs that take bare `Float64Array` positions stay, documented as
Earth-fixed (or relative to their stated parent). New frame-aware overloads
or methods sit beside them.

### 6.3 Moving objects

A `Trajectory` is a frame member whose position is a function of time, read
from published ephemerides:

- **CCSDS OEM** (Artemis II BET rev F: EME2000, Earth-centred, 60 s steps,
  interpolation as the file's header says, Lagrange or Hermite).
- **SPK** (the Artemis II SPK; LRO; DSN station SPKs for completeness).
- EME2000 inputs get the frame-bias rotation to ICRF.
- Each trajectory states its accuracy class: precise ephemeris, or
  approximate (TLE/SGP4, km-class, labelled as such).

### 6.4 Rendering across bodies

One world holds content on several bodies. Each frame, for each view, the
engine composes every body's frame relative to the view's camera at the
view's instant, in float64, and renders camera-relative, as it does today
within one body. Moving a view from the Earth to the Moon changes a root
transform, not the scene; two views of one world can be on different bodies.

- **The view's body** (`View.body`) chooses the sky, atmosphere and
  controllers. The Earth's atmosphere draws only for views where the Earth
  is relevant **(builder's call: the Earth's sky and aerial perspective when
  the camera is inside or near the Earth's atmosphere; black sky elsewhere)**.
- **The Moon in the sky:** today's sky draws a textured Moon disk. When the
  Moon tileset is in the world, the disk is off and the tileset draws at
  every distance (its root tile far away) **(builder's call)**.
- **`3DTILES_ellipsoid`** is read: a tileset's region bounding volumes are
  interpreted on its ellipsoid, and its `body` names the body it belongs to.
- **Earth-only code converted where the examples need it** (the survey found
  about 20 places): horizon culling per body, tile volumes on non-WGS84
  ellipsoids, height clamping per body, a globe-style controller per body,
  pick results naming the body. The globe's tiling stays Earth-only.

### 6.5 Picking, snapping and measurement

**Picking** returns a frame-tagged point: the body, the body-fixed position,
and, when the hit content has an engineering frame, the design coordinates
of that point (section 7.3), plus the feature hit.

**Snapping** follows 1.x's `SnapService` (an interface; view-scoped, since
apertures are CSS pixels and nearest ordering depends on the camera), with
one change: results come back in the design's own frame, mapped by the
frame tree, not through one rigid ECEF matrix. A result holds the snapped
point, the raw hit, how near it is, the geometry type (point, segment, arc,
surface), the normal, and nearby curve geometry. Implementations:

- **Client-side:** snaps to loaded tile geometry (vertices, edges, surface)
  and states its tessellation tolerance.
- **Exact (example):** the synthetic corridor's snapper snaps to the
  analytic geometry, so measurements on it are exact.
- A server adapter (ion's element snap) is possible later; it would need a
  per-element design-frame result rather than ion's rigid `ecefTransform`.

**Measurement** is an engine call taking two points, each in any frame, on
any body or trajectory, at an instant. The result carries every form that
applies:

- **Straight-line distance** (instantaneous, geometric) and the delta vector
  in a chosen frame.
- **Distance over the body** where both points are on one body: the
  ellipsoid geodesic (Karney's algorithm), with the height difference
  stated.
- **Design distance** where both points are in one engineering frame: grid
  distance and deltas in the design's units, plus the combined scale factor
  and grid convergence relating it to the true distance.
- **Station, offset and elevation** of each point where an alignment is
  attached.
- **Signal:** one-way light time in each direction (converged, in BCRS, with
  the solar and Earth Shapiro terms), the light-time range, the range rate
  and the Doppler at a given frequency, and the apparent directions at each
  end (for pointing: a dish receiving points at where the spacecraft was, a
  dish transmitting at where it will be).
- **Line of sight:** clear, or blocked, and by which body (ellipsoid test;
  terrain optional, the builder's call).
- **Accuracy:** an estimate built from the inputs (ephemeris class,
  trajectory class, EOP prediction age, tessellation tolerance, snapped or
  not).

The same call evaluates over a time span at a step, for tracking links as
time runs. Picking two things and measuring is two calls.

## 7. Engineering frames

### 7.1 Definition

An engineering frame holds: the body; the horizontal CRS (EPSG code, WKT or
PROJJSON); the vertical datum (ellipsoidal, geoid or a named datum);
the units (from the CRS definition, never assumed: the US survey foot is not
the international foot); an optional site transform (a local grid inside
the projected system: rotation, translation and a scale, since an iModel
GCS's additional Helmert transform carries one and DOT projects often work
in "ground" coordinates scaled by a project factor); and optional
alignments. It can come from
tileset metadata, from an iModel's geographic coordinate system, or from the
application.

### 7.2 Display: fitting long models to the body

- **Reprojected tiles:** each vertex goes design → CRS → geodetic → body-
  fixed through PROJ when tiles are made, so the model follows the body's
  curvature exactly. For the synthetic corridor this happens in the offline
  generator.
- **Rigid tiles:** placed by one transform at the project center (an
  iModel's `ecefLocation`). Exact at the center, sagging away from it.
- **A tileset declares which it is** (its placement mode), because the
  inverse mapping for measurement depends on it.
- **The declaration follows the 3D Tiles 2.0 draft georeferencing
  extensions** (their CRS and position-on-an-ellipsoid placement), as they
  stand when built. The builder first checks the draft's current names and
  shape. Where it doesn't cover something this design needs (the placement
  mode, the site transform's scale, alignments, the units of a local grid),
  the builder adds the minimum on top, documented as a proposal for the
  draft, and keeps the reader separate so it can follow the draft as it
  changes.
- **A tileset that declares nothing gets no design coordinates.** Its picks
  and measurements report true values only, unless the application supplies
  its engineering frame and placement mode. The engine never guesses a
  placement mode, so it is never silently wrong **(builder's call, from the
  anti-goal)**.
- Per-tile rigid placement from a tangent frame at each tile's center, and
  warping in a shader, are fallbacks the engine doesn't need for the
  examples **(builder's call: not built now)**.

Whether Bentley's 3D Tiles export of a projected iModel reprojects per
vertex or places rigidly is undocumented; a real long iModel, when one is
found, settles it by comparing its vertices at both ends against PROJ.

### 7.3 Measurement agrees with the CAD data

The inverse mapping returns design coordinates for a picked point:

- **Reprojected tiles:** body-fixed → geodetic → CRS (PROJ inverse) →
  design coordinates.
- **Rigid tiles:** the inverse of the rigid transform. The display sags,
  but the tiles are in design coordinates, so the inverse gives the design
  coordinates of what was clicked, exactly.

So measurements agree with the CAD data either way, as long as the engine
knows the placement mode. The round trip design point → display → pick →
design point must return the original to well under 1 mm, anywhere on a
50 km corridor (a test, section 10).

Design distances are grid distances in design units, as the CAD tool
reports them. The true distance over the body is shown beside them and
labelled, never substituted.

### 7.4 PROJ

PROJ compiled to WebAssembly (proj.js, MIT: ~1.3 MB brotli without TIFF,
plus `proj.db` ~1.3 MB brotli, trimmable), loaded lazily the first time an
engineering frame needs an exact transform. Grids (geoids, datum shifts)
are fetched per file as needed and self-hosted if cdn.proj.org lacks CORS.
The EPSG terms of use ship with `proj.db`. proj4js isn't enough (no
vertical datums or geoids) **(builder's call: proj.js; proj-wasm is the
alternative if its worker model suits better)**.

### 7.5 Any body

Engineering frames work on any body, not only the Earth: a lunar site grid
is attached to the Moon's ME frame through a lunar CRS (PROJ's IAU 2015
codes; their axes are taken as ME, the same assumption as Cesium Moon's),
and the forward and inverse mappings are tested on the Moon's sphere as well
as WGS84. The Artemis example includes a hypothetical lunar south-pole
antenna structure in its own site grid (section 8.2), so cross-body
measurements start from design coordinates on the Moon.

### 7.6 Alignments

An alignment is a horizontal geometry (tangents, circular curves, clothoid
spirals) and a vertical profile (grades, parabolic vertical curves), with a
start station. It gives station, offset and elevation for any design point,
exactly, and a point for any station and offset. The synthetic corridor
defines its alignment directly.

## 8. The examples

### 8.1 Design coordinates on the globe

- **A synthetic highway corridor, ~50 km,** in a real projected CRS
  **(builder's call: a US State Plane zone in US survey feet, which also
  exercises the units trap; a UTM variant is easy to add)**, with an
  alignment (tangents, curves, spirals, a vertical profile), stationing,
  and generated geometry: the road surface, shoulders, barriers, a bridge.
  An offline Node script generates it in design coordinates and tiles it
  twice: rigid at the center (as `ecefLocation` does) and reprojected per
  vertex. Its metadata gives the CRS, the placement mode and the alignment.
- **A site-grid model nearby:** a building in a local grid inside the same
  CRS, placed rigidly, to show the machinery costs nothing where curvature
  doesn't matter.
- **A portfolio:** several synthetic sites around the globe in different
  CRSs (UTM zones, State Plane zones, a local grid), and public ion assets
  where available. Bentley's Metrostation through the Sandcastle share key
  is optional (it expires 2027-01-01). A slot is left for the owner's real
  long iModel when found. For real iModels the application supplies the
  engineering frame (CRS, units, site transform, placement mode); fetching
  it from the iTwin Platform API is later work.
- **Interaction:** switch between rigid and reprojected; measure between two
  points (design, true, scale factor); read station, offset and elevation
  along the alignment; a check panel comparing every shown value with the
  generator's exact answer.

### 8.2 Earth to Moon: Artemis II

- **Data:** Artemis II BET rev F (OEM or SPK, NAIF); DE440 and lunar PCK
  excerpts; EOP; the Earth with terrain and imagery; Cesium Moon (ion 2684829)
  under its assumptions (section 11); DSN station positions from DSN 810-005
  (cm-class) and the receive and transmit horizon masks; LRO from JPL
  Horizons (-85) as a real lunar orbiter. A hypothetical lunar south-pole
  antenna structure in its own site grid (section 7.5), labelled
  hypothetical; a hypothetical relay may be added, labelled the same way.
- **Time:** a timeline from 2026-04-02 01:58 UTC (after the upper stage
  separated) to 2026-04-10 23:53 UTC (entry interface), scrubbable, with
  play rates.
- **Outside that span:** the upper stage's RL10 burns (perigee and apogee
  raise, before separation), the service module's break-up and the
  descent with its re-entry plasma (after entry interface). The builder
  first searches for published data (an ICPS trajectory; a descent or
  splashdown trajectory; NASA's airborne entry imagery). Where none exists,
  these are modelled simply from published milestones (burn times and
  results, entry interface speed ~10.7 km/s, the six-minute blackout,
  splashdown at 8:07 pm EDT) and labelled illustrative, kept apart from the
  exact data and excluded from measurements' accuracy claims.
- **Cameras:**
  - follow: orbit freely around Orion as it moves;
  - free flight: intuitive six-axis flight, speed scaled by the distance to
    the nearest body's surface, handing over to that body's controls near
    it;
  - presets: departure after the TLI burn (Earth's limb and atmosphere),
    the far-side flyby, Earthset over the lunar horizon, the loss of signal
    behind the Moon, closest approach, a DSN dish slewing to track Orion
    near a handover between complexes, and entry.
- **Flight path:** past and future, drawn in a chosen frame (the Earth's:
  the figure-eight; the Moon's: the swing around the far side).
- **Coordinate readouts:** Orion and a picked point in Earth-fixed,
  Moon-fixed, Earth-centred inertial and Sun-centred frames.
- **Comms overlay (toggleable),** each quantity marked published, computed
  or assumed:
  - the network: DSN dishes at Goldstone, Canberra and Madrid; links drawn
    while they exist; dish models that track Orion (azimuth and elevation
    from apparent directions);
  - per link, live: range and one-way light time, range rate and Doppler at
    2216.5 MHz, free-space path loss, and received power, G/T, C/N0, Eb/N0
    and margin from published ground figures (DSN 810-005) and assumed
    Orion figures (EIRP ~15 dBW, back-solved from DSN Now readings), with
    the archived DSN Now readings plotted as observed points;
  - elevation against each station's masks; visibility windows and
    handovers between the complexes;
  - the Moon's occultation with loss and acquisition of signal, checked
    against the published 22:44–23:25 UTC on April 6;
  - the data rate as observed (2, 3, 6 Mb/s, sampled), and the O2O laser
    demo as an annotation (its pass times aren't published).
- **Models:** NASA's DSN 34 m and 70 m GLBs and LRO GLB (Draco); Orion with
  its service module from the CC-BY Sketchfab model (the owner downloads it;
  credited), or a fallback from NASA's crew-module mesh; the ICPS from an
  SLS model or modelled simply.
- **Effects (custom shaders), physical by default with a stated exaggeration
  control for the presets:**
  - plumes on proxy geometry, with a vacuum expansion density model
    (ρ ∝ (r*/r)² · cos^(2/(γ−1))(πθ/2θmax); check the form against Simons
    1972): the RL10 nearly invisible with its nozzle extension glowing dull
    red to orange; the OMS-E (TLI, 5 min 49 s at 7:49 pm EDT April 2) a
    faint warm haze with puffs at ignition and shutdown; attitude-thruster
    puffs of 20–200 ms;
  - re-entry plasma: a shock layer ~0.3 m over the heat shield, white-yellow
    at the stagnation point, lavender-pink in the layer, an orange wake and
    trail; the heat shield's blackbody glow at 1,900–3,000 K; the service
    module breaking up separately; the six-minute blackout.

## 10. Validation

- **Time and ephemerides:** reference vectors from jplephem, ERFA, Skyfield
  or astropy, generated offline and checked in as test fixtures; tests
  assert agreement to well under the budget (section 4).
- **Cross-body:** computed Orion ranges against the archived DSN Now ranges
  (to their rounding); the computed far-side occultation against the
  published loss and acquisition of signal times.
- **Design coordinates:** the round trip design → display → pick → design
  under 1 mm everywhere on the corridor; every measurement the corridor
  example shows matches the generator's exact value within a stated
  tolerance; the US survey foot handled.
